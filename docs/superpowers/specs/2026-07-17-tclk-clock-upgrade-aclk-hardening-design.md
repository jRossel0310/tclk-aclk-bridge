# TCLK 5 ns Clock Upgrade + ACLK Rate-Hardening: Design

**Date:** 2026-07-17
**Status:** Approved design, pending implementation plan
**Branch:** `tclk-clock-upgrade`

## Goal

Future-proof the single-board `aclk_pipeline` timestamper in two independent pieces:

- **Part A (TCLK clock upgrade):** run the TCLK event domain at `clk_40m` = 200 MHz / `clk_80m` = 400 MHz, giving **5 ns** timestamp resolution and roughly **5x** TCLK event-rate headroom, with the biphase decoder made functionally correct at the higher oversample rate.
- **Part B (ACLK rate-hardening):** widen the ACLK readout's burst tolerance and confirm the sustained drain ceiling, so the ACLK path absorbs higher event density.

Both parts are proven in cocotb simulation before the bitstream is trusted on hardware. The deliverable is a deployable `uart_echo_bd_wrapper.bit.bin` plus its sim proof.

## Motivation

The lab may see higher event rates in the future, especially on the ACLK decode, and we want the clocking and buffering headroom in place now. The clock-push probes established that the fabric closes timing at these rates on this exact part (`xck26-sfvc784-2LV-c`): 200/400 MHz closed at WNS +0.566 ns and even 250/500 MHz closed at +0.299 ns, so 5 ns is comfortably inside the timing envelope.

**Honest expectation, recorded so the spec carries it:** the measured event-to-event differential sigma (~50.8 ns, $1D vs $0C) is dominated by a decode-latency / generator-scheduling tail, not by the 25 ns quantization. This upgrade delivers finer *quantization* (clean 5 ns bins that resolve the distribution core) and higher *rate headroom*. It does **not** claim to shrink that sigma; a tighter sigma would be a separate "attack the tail" investigation. The justification here is resolution and future rate headroom, not sigma.

## Background: two independent clock domains

The pipeline has two decode paths on two unrelated clocks. This matters because the upgrade only touches one of them.

- **TCLK path:** `clk_80m` (serdec oversample) and `clk_40m` (deserializer + readout + `wr_timebase`) are generated by an in-PL clocking wizard (`clk_wiz_0`) from `pl_clk0`. These are the clocks Part A raises. The TCLK `wr_timebase` counts on `clk_40m`, so `clk_40m` sets the timestamp resolution.
- **ACLK path:** the ACLK decoder and its `wr_timebase` run on `rx_usrclk2` (~62.5 MHz), which the GT transceiver recovers from the 1.25 Gbps line. It is line-locked, not generated by `clk_wiz`, so the Part A clock upgrade does not affect it. ACLK's timestamp resolution is therefore fixed at 16 ns (one 8b10b word) and is out of scope here. ACLK rate headroom is a FIFO-depth and drain-throughput question, which is Part B.

## Part A: TCLK clock upgrade

### A1. Clock generation (`clk_wiz_0`)

In `vivado/build_aclk_pipeline.tcl`, set the two event-domain clock outputs:

- `CLKOUT1_REQUESTED_OUT_FREQ` 80.000 -> **400.000** (`clk_80m`)
- `CLKOUT2_REQUESTED_OUT_FREQ` 40.000 -> **200.000** (`clk_40m`)

The 2:1 ratio is preserved, so the XDC async clock groups (`clk_out*clk_wiz*` wildcard) need no change. The MMCM feasibility of 400/200 from ~100 MHz was confirmed during the probes. The clock signal names `clk_80m` / `clk_40m` are kept as generic domain labels (they now carry 400/200 in this build); renaming them across the BD, XDC, and TCL would be pure churn and is out of scope.

### A2. Serdec oversample parameterization (the crux)

`rtl/aclk_bridge/serdec4_9MHz.v` oversamples the raw biphase-mark TCLK line on `clk_80m`. Its delay-line taps are hardcoded for an oversample ratio of 8 samples per 100 ns TCLK bit-cell (80 MHz). At 400 MHz that ratio is 40, so the taps must be rescaled or the decode is garbage.

**Approach:** parameterize the module by `OSR` (oversample ratio = `clk_80m` frequency / 10 MHz), and derive every TCLK-cell-referenced constant from `OSR`:

- **Decode-critical taps (must be exact):** the delayed-edge reference `TCLK_del[7]/[8]` becomes `TCLK_del[OSR-1]/[OSR]`. The immediate-edge detect `TCLK_del[1]/[2]` marks "now" (a small fixed detection pipeline, not cell-referenced), but its 2-sample window is ~5 ns of glitch rejection at 400 MHz versus ~25 ns today, so its width is re-evaluated to preserve the current physical debounce on the real line rather than blindly kept at `[1]/[2]` (see Risks: real-line edge-noise). The `TCLK_del` shift register widens from 13 bits to approximately `1.5*OSR + margin` bits (about 61 bits at OSR=40, set by the longest `sig_err` run-length tap) so every referenced tap fits. The plan pins the exact tap indices and register width via TDD.
- **`sig_err` run-length patterns:** the illegal-run-length comparisons (`TCLK_del[3:1]`, `[4:1]`, `[8:1]`, `[12:1]`, etc.) become `OSR`-derived. These are **diagnostic only** (the decode FSM does not consume `sig_err`), so they are lower-risk, and importantly they are freely pipelineable if they threaten timing (see A5).
- **Default `OSR = 8`.** Every existing consumer (the standalone `tclk` and other builds, the existing cocotb harness) instantiates the default and stays bit-identical.

The serdec-to-deserializer interface (`SCLK` / `SDATA`, from `clk_80m` into the `clk_40m` deserializer) is unaffected: the deserializer already 2-FF synchronizes and edge-detects those signals as an asynchronous crossing (`TCLK_DESERIALIZER2.v`), and because the 2:1 ratio is preserved, the deserializer sees the same cycle-count relationship as today. Only the serdec's taps relative to the external 10 MHz TCLK change.

### A3. TCLK `wr_timebase` constants

In `rtl/aclk_pipeline_bd_top.v`, the TCLK `wr_timebase` instance (`u_tb_tclk`) currently assumes 40 MHz. Update it for 200 MHz:

- `CLK_PERIOD_DS` 250 -> **50** (5.0 ns period). Without this the interpolated nanoseconds ramp about 5x too fast between WR edges.
- `CLK10_TIMEOUT` 16 -> **80** (keeps the physical ~400 ns 10 MHz watchdog window). At 200 MHz the old value of 16 cycles is 80 ns, shorter than the 100 ns gap between WR 10 MHz edges, so the watchdog would expire every cell and the timebase would never lock.
- `PPS_TIMEOUT` 44,000,000 -> **220,000,000** (keeps the physical ~1.1 s PPS watchdog window at 200 MHz).

The ACLK `wr_timebase` instance (`u_tb_aclk`, `CLK_PERIOD_DS = 160`) and the `wr_timebase_axi` monitor (on the unchanged 100 MHz AXI clock) are not touched.

### A4. Isolation

Because `OSR` defaults to 8 and only `aclk_pipeline` instantiates `OSR = 40`, no other design or consumer changes behavior. The ACLK path, the AXI/`pl_clk0` domain, and the GT domain are untouched.

### A5. Timing closure and the `sig_err` contingency

The 400/200 probe closed at WNS +0.566 ns, but with the *old* 80 MHz serdec. The functional serdec is larger (wider `TCLK_del`, a bigger `sig_err` comparison cloud), so 400 MHz timing must be re-verified after the rewrite. If WNS goes negative, the mitigation is to **pipeline the `sig_err` diagnostic** (register the wide comparisons across one or more `clk_80m` stages). `sig_err` is not on the decode path, so adding latency to it carries no functional risk. This contingency is part of the plan, not a surprise.

## Part B: ACLK rate-hardening

Current state (already in place from earlier work): the ACLK readout FIFO is **already 512 deep** (`aclk_gt_readout_top` instantiated at `ADDR_WIDTH = 9`), and the PS drain path is the optimized shared `readout_common` (ctypes single-store pop, cached views), used by both sources. The readout core already exposes a sticky overflow bit in its `STATUS` register.

### B1. Deeper burst FIFO

Raise the ACLK readout FIFO from 512 to **2048** (`ADDR_WIDTH` 9 -> 11) in the `aclk_gt_readout_top` instantiation in `rtl/aclk_pipeline_bd_top.v`, for roughly 4x more burst absorption. This is timing-safe because the ACLK FIFO write side runs at 62.5 MHz, well below the point where the distributed-RAM (LUTRAM) async-read FIFO became the TCLK timing wall at 250 MHz.

**Utilization guard:** the FIFO is distributed RAM, so 2048 deep is LUT-heavy. Implementation must check LUTRAM utilization after synthesis. If 2048 is too costly, fall back to 1024 (`ADDR_WIDTH` 10), and record BRAM-backed FIFO (a registered-read variant of `async_fifo`) as a **follow-up**, not part of this build.

### B2. Sustained drain confirmation

A deeper FIFO only extends how long a *burst* can outrun the reader; **sustained** higher rates are bounded by PS drain throughput. Implementation must measure the current post-optimization ACLK drain/publish ceiling and confirm the readout overflow bit stays clear at the target sustained rate. If the anticipated sustained rate exceeds the drain ceiling, that is flagged as a software-drain problem (the real lever), with the measured headroom quantified, rather than silently assuming the FIFO fixes it.

### B3. Honest limiter note

ACLK resolution stays 16 ns (line-locked). Part B buys burst tolerance and a measured sustained-rate margin; it does not raise ACLK's decode clock, which is not a `clk_wiz` lever.

## Validation

### Cocotb simulation

The TX model `tb/tclk_tx_model.py` is already parameterized by `SAMPLES_PER_CELL` (= `OSR`), so validating at the new rate is a knob, not a harness rewrite.

- **Regression (proves the rewrite is safe):** the existing `tb/tclk_rcv` and `tb/tclk_readout` suites at `OSR = 8` (`SAMPLES_PER_CELL = 8`, clocks 80/40) must still pass bit-identically after the serdec parameterization.
- **New target (proves 400 MHz decode):** the same suites at `OSR = 40` (`SAMPLES_PER_CELL = 40`, clocks 400/200). Acceptance: known event codes decode in order, a bad-parity frame raises PERR / one `ERROR_COUNT` and never yields an event, timestamps strictly increasing, `EVENT_COUNT` exact, no false overflow.
- **ACLK depth:** the shared readout-core suite (`tb/tclk_readout`, which exercises the same `async_fifo` / `aclk_readout_axi` core) run at `ADDR_WIDTH = 11`, plus an explicit overflow-behavior check (fill past depth, confirm the sticky overflow bit sets and no silent loss).

### Build and timing

Build with `hw.ps1` (the Windows Defender exclusion for `C:\Xilinx` and the repo `build` tree is now in place, so the IP-Integrator flake is resolved). After implementation, re-verify post-route WNS at 400/200 with the functional serdec; apply the A5 `sig_err`-pipelining contingency if needed. Timing acceptance: WNS >= 0, 0 failed nets, hold met.

## Risks and mitigations

- **Serdec tap-scaling correctness (highest risk).** Mitigated by the `OSR = 8` bit-identical regression plus the `OSR = 40` end-to-end decode proof against the real-timing biphase TX model.
- **Real-line edge-noise rejection.** The clean cocotb decode proof injects no edge jitter or glitches, so it cannot confirm that the finer 400 MHz sample period still rejects short line glitches (the immediate-edge tap's physical debounce shrinks ~5x if left at `[1]/[2]`). Note the `OSR = 8` regression cannot catch this either, since `[1]/[2]` is unchanged there. Mitigated by sizing the immediate-edge window to preserve today's physical debounce, optionally a jitter/glitch-injected `OSR = 40` decode variant, and confirmed at board bring-up on the live line.
- **400 MHz timing closure with the larger serdec.** Mitigated by pipelining the diagnostic `sig_err` cloud (A5).
- **ACLK LUTRAM utilization at 2048 deep.** Mitigated by the post-synth utilization guard and the 1024 fallback (B1).
- **Board bring-up.** The deliverable is sim-validated plus a deployable bitstream. Live-TCLK board verification (confirm decode + timebase lock + resolution on the real line) is a follow-up the operator runs; implementation will provide the exact check commands.

## Out of scope

- ACLK timestamp resolution (stays 16 ns, line-locked).
- BRAM-backed FIFO re-architecture (only a follow-up if the LUTRAM guard trips).
- Any investigation or reduction of the decode-latency / scheduling tail (the sigma driver). This build is resolution and rate, not sigma.
- Renaming the `clk_80m` / `clk_40m` signals.
- The GT line rate and the ACLK decode clock.

## Deliverables

1. Parameterized `serdec4_9MHz.v` (`OSR`, default 8), with the OSR=8 regression and OSR=40 decode proof green.
2. `aclk_pipeline_bd_top.v` with `u_tb_tclk` constants updated and the ACLK FIFO deepened.
3. `build_aclk_pipeline.tcl` at 400/200.
4. A deployable `uart_echo_bd_wrapper.bit.bin` with WNS >= 0 recorded.
5. The measured ACLK drain-ceiling / overflow confirmation.
6. Operator bring-up check commands for the board.

## Sequencing

Two independently-testable pieces. Do **Part B first** (low-risk FIFO depth bump plus drain measurement) then **Part A** (the serdec rewrite, where the care goes). One plan, sequenced so a failure in the risky part does not block the easy win.
